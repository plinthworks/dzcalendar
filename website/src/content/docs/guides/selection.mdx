A selection is a stretch of empty time that a person marks out — to book it,
to block it, to start an event from it. The calendar draws it and tells you
what it covers; it creates nothing. What the selection becomes is your
decision. This guide covers making one, using it, and telling it from a click.

## Let a person select

Turn it on with [`selectable`](../../options/selectable/). Nothing else is
needed:

- **With a mouse or a pen**, press on empty time and drag. In a time grid that
  selects hours, in a month whole days. A plain click is not a selection: it
  is a date click, described below. A press on an event is about the event (its
  click, its drag), never the time behind it.
- **With a finger**, a tap on empty time selects one slot, with a handle at
  each end to stretch it. A finger that moves from empty space scrolls the
  page, as it should: the handles are how a touch screen extends a selection.
- **With the keyboard**, a section of its own comes further down.

Hours snap to a step, [`snapMinutes`](../../options/snap-minutes/) (15 by
default), the same one that drags, resizes and the arrow keys use. Days in a
month are whole days whatever it says.

Try it: drag across empty hours; click on empty time to clear it.

## Turn a selection into an event

When a person finishes a selection, [`onSelect`](../../options/on-select/)
receives it: `{ start, end, allDay, resourceId? }`. A timed selection has
`ZonedDateTime`s in the calendar's zone; an all-day one has dates, the `end`
being the day **after** the last one — the same convention as an all-day
event, so the selection can be handed to `event.create` as it is.

The selection stays drawn while you ask what to do with it. Clear it when you
are done: in plain JavaScript `calendar.select(null)`; in Angular, React and
Vue by setting the two-way `selection` to `null`, from `onSelect` itself if
you like.

<CodeTabs>
<Fragment slot="js">

```ts
import { createCalendar } from '@dzcalendar/dom';

const calendar = createCalendar(element, {
  timeZone: 'Europe/Paris',
  selectable: true,
  snapMinutes: 30,
  onSelect: (selection) => {
    const title = prompt('Title?');
    if (title) {
      calendar.run({
        type: 'event.create',
        event: { title, start: selection.start, end: selection.end, allDay: selection.allDay },
      });
    }
    calendar.select(null);
  },
});
```

</Fragment>

```ts
import type { Selection } from '@dzcalendar/core';
import { DZ_CALENDAR, DzCalendar } from '@dzcalendar/angular';

@Component({
  selector: 'app-agenda',
  imports: [DZ_CALENDAR],
  template: `
    <dz-calendar
      timeZone="Europe/Paris"
      [selectable]="true"
      [options]="{ snapMinutes: 30 }"
      [(selection)]="selection"
      (selected)="book($event)" />`,
})
export class Agenda {
  private readonly cal = viewChild.required(DzCalendar);
  readonly selection = signal<Selection | null>(null);

  book(selection: Selection): void {
    const title = prompt('Title?');
    if (title) {
      this.cal().controller.dispatch({
        type: 'event.create',
        event: { title, start: selection.start, end: selection.end, allDay: selection.allDay },
      });
    }
    this.selection.set(null);
  }
}
```

Two details worth knowing:

- **`onSelect` hears a person, not your code.** A selection you show with
  `calendar.select(selection)` is drawn, and not said back.
- **`onUnselect` hears the selection go by itself**: a click on empty time in
  the calendar or a press anywhere outside it, Escape, a move to another period or view, a new
  drag. It does not hear you clear it. A form of yours beside the calendar keeps the
  selection while it is filled in when it carries `data-dz-keep-selection`. Use it to close a form that was open for the selection —
  see [`onUnselect`](../../options/on-unselect/); the two-way `selection` of
  the frameworks becomes `null` in the same cases.

## Select with the keyboard

A pointer drags; a keyboard has nothing to drag, so it grows a selection
instead. With the focus in the grid and a [`selectable`](../../options/selectable/)
calendar:

- **Enter or Space on a slot** is a date click and also selects that one slot,
  which then reaches `onSelect`. (A mouse click does not select; the keyboard
  has no drag, so its "tap" does.)
- **Shift and the arrows** grow the selection from where Shift started. Each
  step is said to a screen reader ("Selected: Wednesday 21 October, 09:00 to
  10:00"), and the same snap and the same hours apply as for a pointer.
- **Enter** (or Space) gives the selection to `onSelect`. In a month, Enter on a day that
  holds events goes into them instead — unless a selection is being made.
- **An arrow without Shift**, or **Escape**, clears it (`onUnselect`; Escape
  says "Selection cleared").

Everything the keyboard does in the calendar is in
[Keyboard and accessibility](../keyboard-accessibility/).

## A click is not a selection

Four things can start from empty time, and each has its own callback:

| The person | The calendar calls |
|---|---|
| Clicks a day or an empty slot | [`onDateClick`](../../options/on-date-click/) — `{ allDay, date, instant?, resourceId? }`, nothing drawn |
| Drags across empty time, or taps on a touch screen | [`onSelect`](../../options/on-select/) — a range, left drawn |
| Clicks a free slot you offered with [`availableSlots`](../../options/available-slots/) | [`onSlotClick`](../../options/on-slot-click/) — the slot as you gave it |
| Chooses "New event at …" in the context menu | [`onCreateRequest`](../../options/on-create-request/) — `{ allDay, start, resourceId? }` |

A date click needs no `selectable`. In a time grid its `instant` is the start
of the slot clicked, on `snapMinutes`: a click at 10:37 says 10:30. On a touch
screen a tap is both: a date click, then a one-slot selection.

A free slot is a button you drew on purpose, so when `onSlotClick` is given a
press on it is the slot's — not a date click, not the start of a drag. Without
it, slots are only shown, and a press on one behaves as on empty time.

"New event at …" ("New all-day event on …" in a month) is the right-click,
long-press or Menu-key way of starting an event. Give `onCreateRequest` and the
menu offers it, snapped to `snapMinutes`, on a
day or at a moment; it is a point, where a selection is a range. Both can open
the same editor.

## Limits and refusals

A selection is **not** held to [`rules`](../../options/rules/) or to
[`businessHours`](../../options/business-hours/). Those check what a person
does to an *event* — move it, resize it, drop it — and a refused gesture is
said by [`onProposalRefused`](../../options/on-proposal-refused/). Selecting
time moves no event, so nothing is refused: the closed hours are shaded in the
grid, and a person can still select them.

When a selection must respect a limit, decide in `onSelect` and clear it. This
one accepts only a single day's stretch inside the weekday opening hours of
`businessHours: true` (Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 17:00):

<CodeTabs>
<Fragment slot="js">

```ts
import { Temporal } from '@dzcalendar/core';
import { createCalendar } from '@dzcalendar/dom';

const calendar = createCalendar(element, {
  timeZone: 'Europe/Paris',
  selectable: true,
  businessHours: true,
  onSelect: ({ start, end }) => {
    const open =
      start instanceof Temporal.ZonedDateTime &&
      end instanceof Temporal.ZonedDateTime &&
      start.toPlainDate().equals(end.toPlainDate()) &&
      start.dayOfWeek <= 5 &&
      start.hour >= 9 &&
      end.hour * 60 + end.minute <= 17 * 60;
    if (open) return console.log(`book ${start.toString()} → ${end.toString()}`);
    alert('Outside opening hours');
    calendar.select(null);
  },
});
```

</Fragment>
</CodeTabs>

For bookings, the sturdier way is not to let a person select at all, but to
offer the free slots with [`availableSlots`](../../options/available-slots/)
and hear [`onSlotClick`](../../options/on-slot-click/): only what you offered
can be chosen, and your server checks it again before saving. Whatever the
way, validate on the server too: a selection is only a proposal.

An event the person then creates and drags is held to the rules like any
other: see [Drag and drop](../drag-and-drop/).

## Show and clear a selection from code

`calendar.select(selection)` draws a selection as if a person had made it,
without calling `onSelect` — to preselect a slot from a list, or to mark what
a form is about. `calendar.select(null)` clears it, without calling
`onUnselect`. Pass the same shape `onSelect` gives:

```ts
import { Temporal } from '@dzcalendar/core';

calendar.select({
  allDay: false,
  start: Temporal.ZonedDateTime.from('2026-10-21T14:00[Europe/Paris]'),
  end: Temporal.ZonedDateTime.from('2026-10-21T15:00[Europe/Paris]'),
});
```

A selection belongs to the period it was made in: going to another week or
view clears it. In the frameworks, the two-way `selection` is the same handle,
read and written.

## In each view

- **A month, or any grid of days**: whole days. Dragging from the 21st to the
  23rd gives `start` the 21st and `end` the 24th, `allDay: true`.
- **A time grid**: hours, snapped to `snapMinutes`. Dragging up or down inside
  a day gives a stretch of that day; dragging into another day's column gives
  one stretch of time from the first press to the last. Dragging into the
  all-day row at the top selects whole days instead.
- **A resource view**: the selection carries the `resourceId` of the column or
  row where it started — the Pro's timeline works the same way along its time
  axis, one resource per row. Read it to know whose time was chosen.
- **The list and the year** are for reading, not for selecting time.

## Across a clock change

A selection is made of wall-clock slots, read in the pass under the pointer,
so it is the right length on a day of 23, 25 or 23 and a half hours. Its
`start` and `end` carry their offsets: measure it with `start.until(end)` and
you get the real time, not the difference of two clock readings.

At a change, the slot grid follows the wall clock. In Paris, at 02:00 on the
last Sunday of March, the hour 02:00–03:00 does not exist: it is drawn as a
band, a pointer in it counts as 03:00, the first moment after it, and a
selection from 01:00 to 03:00 is one hour long. On the last Sunday of October,
02:00–03:00 happens twice: the grid draws it once, a pointer in it reads the
first pass, and a selection across it holds both — from 01:00 to 03:00 is
three hours, its `end` at `03:00+01:00`. The keyboard steps through both
passes; [`timeGrid`](../../options/time-grid/)'s `axis: 'elapsed'` draws
them one after the other.

Lord Howe, off Australia, shows the half hour: on Sunday 4 October 2026 its
clocks go from 02:00 to 02:30. With a one-hour `snapMinutes`, a tap at 02:40
selects 02:30–03:00 with `skipped: true` — the slot's start, 02:00, does not
exist, and the selection still holds the moment that was tapped. Try dragging across 02:00 on that Sunday:
